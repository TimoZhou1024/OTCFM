\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Multi-view clustering (MVC) aims to partition samples into distinct groups by integrating complementary information from multiple heterogeneous data sources, and has attracted significant attention in machine learning and data mining communities \cite{chaoSurveyMultiviewClustering2021,chowdhuryDeepMultiviewClustering2025}. However, most existing MVC methods \cite{xuMultilevelFeatureLearning2022,zhangLetDataChoose2023,renDynamicWeightedGraph2024} are designed for {ideal} multi-view data, which assumes that all views are {complete} (every sample has observations in all views) and {aligned} (samples across different views have known one-to-one correspondences). In real-world applications, such ideal assumptions are often violated. For instance, in medical diagnosis, a patient may have CT scans but lack MRI images due to cost or availability constraints, resulting in {incomplete} views \cite{linCOMPLETERIncompleteMultiview2021}. 
In social media analysis, user profiles across different platforms (e.g., Twitter and LinkedIn) often lack explicit linking, creating naturally unaligned multi-view scenarios \cite{xinMultilevelReliableGuidance2025}. Consequently, the problem of clustering multi-view data under simultaneous incompleteness and misalignment remains largely unresolved, posing a fundamental challenge to existing multi-view learning paradigms. %%In cross-lingual document clustering, different language versions of documents may not have explicit correspondence annotations, leading to \textit{unaligned} views \cite{huangPartiallyViewalignedClustering2020a}. Similarly,  How to effectively cluster such non-ideal multi-view data---characterized by incompleteness and misalignment---has emerged as a critical research challenge.

To address these challenges, recent studies have increasingly focused on developing effective methods for mining non-ideal multi-view data, thereby enabling robust multi-view clustering. 
For the \textit{incomplete} multi-view scenario (IMVC), contrastive learning-based methods \cite{linCOMPLETERIncompleteMultiview2021,yangRobustMultiViewClustering2023,jinDeepIncompleteMultiView2023} dominate recent literature, which recover missing view representations by maximizing cross-view feature consistency through instance-level or prototype-level alignment. More recently, Zhang et al.\ \cite{zhangIncompleteMultiviewClustering2025a} introduced diffusion models to IMVC, leveraging conditional denoising to generate high-fidelity missing views while enhancing clustering compactness. For the \textit{unaligned} multi-view scenario (UMVC), Huang et al.\ \cite{huangPartiallyViewalignedClustering2020a} pioneered the permutation learning paradigm, formulating cross-view alignment as learning a differentiable matching network. Subsequent works \cite{xinMultilevelReliableGuidance2025,guoRobustContrastiveMultiview2024a} further improve robustness by exploiting multi-level structural guidance or noise-tolerant contrastive mechanisms to handle noisy and unpaired correspondences.

Subsequent works \cite{xinMultilevelReliableGuidance2025,guoRobustContrastiveMultiview2024a} further improve the robustness of multi-view clustering by incorporating multi-level structural guidance or noise-tolerant contrastive mechanisms, enabling effective clustering in the presence of noisy and unpaired correspondences.

Although these methods have achieved promising performance in incomplete or unaligned multi-view clustering, they still suffer from several critical limitations. \textbf{First}, most existing methods treat incomplete and unaligned scenarios as separate problems, employing distinct pipelines for view imputation and correspondence alignment. This two-stage paradigm inevitably leads to error propagation between stages and suboptimal multi-view fusion. \textbf{Second}, many generative approaches, such as diffusion-based IMVC methods \cite{zhangIncompleteMultiviewClustering2025a}, rely on iterative refinement to reconstruct missing views, limiting their efficiency and responsiveness in dynamic or large-scale multi-view scenarios.%recent diffusion-based IMVC methods \cite{zhangIncompleteMultiviewClustering2025a} achieve high-fidelity view generation but require hundreds of iterative denoising steps, resulting in $O(N \cdot r \cdot V \cdot T)$ forward passes for imputing missing views---a prohibitive latency for real-time MVC applications where rapid clustering updates are essential.
\textbf{Third}, most existing UMVC methods \cite{huangPartiallyViewalignedClustering2020a,xinMultilevelReliableGuidance2025} require explicit correspondence supervision or annotated sample pairs, which is expensive or infeasible when cross-view correspondences are inherently unknown. 

To address the aforementioned limitations, we propose \textbf{OPTION} (\textbf{OP}timal \textbf{T}ransport–Gu\textbf{I}ded Fl\textbf{O}w Matchi\textbf{N}g), a unified framework that tackles all three challenges within a single end-to-end pipeline. Specifically, \textbf{first}, we integrate generation, alignment, and clustering into a unified objective function, where the flow matching module, GW alignment, and soft clustering are jointly optimized end-to-end, eliminating inter-stage error propagation. \textbf{Second}, we employ conditional flow matching \cite{lipmanFlowMatchingGenerative2023a} to learn deterministic transport paths for missing-view imputation, enabling stable manifold-preserving recovery through ODE-based generation with only 10 integration steps---achieving 10-100$\times$ speedup over diffusion models. \textbf{Third}, we introduce a Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) structural coupling loss that aligns intra-view geometric structures in the latent space \textit{without requiring sample correspondences}, enabling alignment-free multi-view fusion for unaligned scenarios. Furthermore, an optional contrastive regularization is incorporated to enhance cross-view consistency specifically for aligned settings.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose OPTION, a unified framework that addresses both incomplete and unaligned multi-view clustering through flow matching, achieving deterministic inference with significantly lower latency than diffusion-based methods.
    \item We develop a Gromov-Wasserstein structural coupling loss that enforces cross-view geometric consistency \textit{without requiring sample correspondences}, enabling true alignment-free multi-view learning.
    \item We introduce a modular architecture that adaptively activates contrastive learning for aligned data while relying on GW-based structural alignment for unaligned scenarios.
    \item Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that OPTION achieves competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods while offering 10-100$\times$ faster inference.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{related-work}

\textbf{Incomplete MVC and Generative Models.} 
Handling missing views is a pivotal challenge in MVC. Early approaches relied on matrix factorization or graph learning to impute missing information implicitly \cite{yangRobustMultiViewClustering2023}. With the advent of deep learning, generative models have become mainstream. Methods like COMPLETER \cite{linCOMPLETERIncompleteMultiview2021} and DeepIMVC \cite{yangDealMVCDualContrastive2023} utilize contrastive learning and prototype alignment to recover missing representations. More recently, Diffusion Models (DMs) \cite{hoDenoisingDiffusionProbabilistic2020b, limScorebasedGenerativeModeling2023} have been introduced to MVC for their superior generation quality. For instance, DCG \cite{zhangIncompleteMultiviewClustering2025a} employs conditional diffusion to generate missing views and enhance clustering compactness. Despite their success, diffusion-based methods suffer from high inference latency due to the iterative denoising process (requiring hundreds of steps) and typically rely on paired data for training, limiting their efficiency and applicability in real-time scenarios.

\textbf{Unaligned Multi-View Learning.} 
Most MVC algorithms assume a strict one-to-one correspondence across views. When this assumption is violated (i.e., unaligned MVC), performance degrades significantly. Existing solutions usually formulate alignment as a permutation learning problem. PVC \cite{huangPartiallyViewalignedClustering2020a} and its successors employ differentiable matching networks to infer correspondences. Recent works like MRG-UMC \cite{xinMultilevelReliableGuidance2025} and CANDY \cite{guoRobustContrastiveMultiview2024a} introduce multi-level guidance and robust contrastive learning to handle noisy or unpaired data. However, these methods often involve solving the assignment problem (e.g., via the Hungarian algorithm), which scales poorly ($O(N^3)$) with dataset size. Furthermore, they typically treat alignment and clustering as separate stages or alternating objectives, lacking a unified framework to handle structural misalignment implicitly.

Rectified Flow \cite{liu2023rectifiedflow} and related works further optimize the transport path to be straight, enabling fast sampling with few-step ODE solvers. The connection between FM and Optimal Transport (OT) provides a geometric perspective on distribution alignment. While OT has been applied to domain adaptation, its integration with Flow Matching for unsupervised multi-view clustering—specifically to address incompleteness and misalignment simultaneously—remains unexplored. Our work bridges this gap by leveraging OT-guided flows for efficient imputation and alignment-free fusion.

\section{Proposed Method}\label{method}

% \textbf{Preliminaries.} Flow Matching (FM) \cite{lipmanFlowMatchingGenerative2023a} learns a deterministic continuous normalizing flow (CNF) by regressing a vector field $v_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t)$ that transports samples from noise $p_0$ to data $p_1$ via ODE integration. \subsection{Problem Formulation and Framework Overview}\label{problem-formulation}

Consider a multi-view dataset $\mathcal{X} = \{ \mathbf{X}^{(v)} \}_{v=1}^V$ with $V$ views. A binary mask $\mathbf{M} \in \{0,1\}^{N \times V}$ indicates view availability (\textbf{Incomplete MVC}), and cross-view sample correspondences may be unknown (\textbf{Unaligned MVC}). Our goal is to jointly: (1) impute missing views, (2) align cross-view geometric structures without explicit correspondences, and (3) discover cluster assignments---all within a unified end-to-end framework.

To achieve this, OPTION adopts a \textit{unified generation-alignment-clustering} paradigm with three core components: (1) \textbf{Latent Embedding} projects heterogeneous views into a shared low-dimensional space via view-specific encoder-decoders; (2) \textbf{Gromov-Wasserstein Alignment} enforces cross-view geometric consistency without sample correspondences; (3) \textbf{Conditional Flow Matching} enables efficient missing-view imputation with only 10 ODE steps. \subsection{Latent Space Construction}\label{latent-space}

To address the high dimensionality and heterogeneity of multi-view data, we project all views into a unified low-dimensional latent space. For each view $v$, a neural encoder $f_{\phi_v}: \mathbb{R}^{d_v} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_z}$ maps the observation $\mathbf{x}_i^{(v)}$ to a latent representation $\mathbf{z}_i^{(v)} = f_{\phi_v}(\mathbf{x}_i^{(v)})$. Symmetric decoders $g_{\psi_v}: \mathbb{R}^{d_z} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_v}$ reconstruct the original views, providing a reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{rec} = \sum_v \|\mathbf{x}^{(v)} - g_{\psi_v}(\mathbf{z}^{(v)})\|^2$ that regularizes the latent space. This design reduces computational complexity from $O(d_v^2)$ to $O(d_z^2)$ for flow matching operations while enabling meaningful cross-view comparisons in the shared space $\mathbb{R}^{d_z}$.

\subsection{Gromov-Wasserstein Structural Alignment}\label{gw-vector-field}

\textbf{Motivation.} A central challenge in unaligned multi-view clustering is that the $i$-th sample in view $v$ does not correspond to the $i$-th sample in view $u$. Consequently, conventional alignment strategies---such as contrastive learning or direct feature averaging---are fundamentally inapplicable, as they require explicit sample correspondences. To overcome this limitation, we introduce a \textit{structural alignment} mechanism based on the Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) distance. Unlike standard Wasserstein distance that measures point-to-point transport cost, GW compares two distributions by examining the \textit{preservation of pairwise relationships}: if two samples are close in one view, their counterparts (under an optimal coupling) should also be close in another view. This relational perspective enables cross-view alignment without knowing which samples correspond.

\textbf{Kernel Matrix Construction.} To capture the geometric structure of each view, we construct a pairwise similarity matrix. For each view $v$, we compute an RBF (Radial Basis Function) kernel matrix $\mathbf{K}^{(v)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times B}$ within a mini-batch of size $B$:
\[
K_{ij}^{(v)} = \exp\left(-\gamma \| \mathbf{z}_i^{(v)} - \mathbf{z}_j^{(v)} \|^2\right)
\]
where $\gamma > 0$ is a bandwidth parameter. The entry $K_{ij}^{(v)}$ quantifies the similarity between the $i$-th and $j$-th samples within view $v$, encoding the local manifold geometry. This kernel formulation is robust to feature scale variations across heterogeneous views.

\textbf{Structural Alignment Loss.} The core insight is that if two views capture the same underlying cluster structure, their kernel matrices should exhibit similar patterns---even when sample indices are shuffled. We formalize this via the GW-inspired loss:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{GW} = \frac{2}{V(V-1)} \sum_{v < u} \| \mathbf{K}^{(v)} - \mathbf{K}^{(u)} \|_F^2
\]
This loss penalizes discrepancies between the pairwise relationship structures across all view pairs. Intuitively, minimizing $\mathcal{L}_{GW}$ encourages the encoder to learn representations such that the \textit{intra-view geometric structure} becomes consistent across views, thereby achieving implicit alignment without explicit correspondences. Importantly, this batch-wise formulation reduces computational complexity from $O(N^3)$ (full GW optimization) to $O(B^2)$ per mini-batch, making it scalable for large datasets.

\subsection{Conditional Flow Matching for Imputation}\label{conditional-flow}

\textbf{Motivation.} Missing view imputation is essential for incomplete multi-view clustering, as downstream fusion and clustering require complete representations. While diffusion models have shown strong generative capabilities, their iterative denoising process (typically $T=100\sim1000$ steps) incurs prohibitive latency. Flow Matching (FM) \cite{lipmanFlowMatchingGenerative2023a} offers an efficient alternative by learning a \textit{deterministic} continuous normalizing flow (CNF) that directly transports samples from a simple prior to the data distribution. The key advantage is that FM enables few-step generation (e.g., 10 steps) while maintaining high sample quality, making it ideal for real-time MVC applications.

\textbf{Flow Matching Formulation.} FM learns a time-dependent vector field $v_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t)$ that defines an ordinary differential equation (ODE): $\frac{d\mathbf{z}_t}{dt} = v_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t)$. By integrating this ODE from $t=0$ to $t=1$, samples are transported from noise $\mathbf{z}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ to data $\mathbf{z}_1 \sim p_{data}$. Following \cite{lipmanFlowMatchingGenerative2023a}, we adopt optimal transport (OT) paths, which yield straight trajectories $\mathbf{z}_t = (1-t)\mathbf{z}_0 + t\mathbf{z}_1$. The target vector field along this path is simply $u_t = \mathbf{z}_1 - \mathbf{z}_0$, and the network is trained to regress this target.

\textbf{Conditional Generation.} For missing view imputation, we extend FM to a \textit{conditional} setting where generation is guided by available semantic information. We learn a conditional vector field $v_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t, \mathbf{c})$ where $\mathbf{c}$ provides context about the target sample. The conditioning strategy differs based on the alignment scenario: For \textbf{aligned (IMVC)} data where sample correspondences are known, we use the consensus representation $\mathbf{c}_i = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}_{avail}^{(i)}|} \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{avail}^{(i)}} \mathbf{z}_i^{(v)}$ aggregated from available views, providing direct semantic guidance. For \textbf{unaligned (UMVC)} data where correspondences are unknown, cross-sample averaging is invalid; instead, we condition on the cluster centroid $\mathbf{c}_i^{(v)} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_{k^*}$ where $k^* = \argmax_k q_{ik}^{(v)}$ is determined by soft assignment, ensuring semantically coherent generation while each view operates independently---cross-view consistency is enforced solely through $\mathcal{L}_{GW}$.

\textbf{Flow Matching Loss.} The training objective minimizes the discrepancy between the predicted and target vector fields:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{CFM} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{z}_0, \mathbf{z}_1, \mathbf{c}} \left[ \| v_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t, \mathbf{c}) - (\mathbf{z}_1 - \mathbf{z}_0) \|^2 \right]
\]
where $t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]$, $\mathbf{z}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, and $\mathbf{z}_1$ is the encoded latent of an observed view.

\textbf{Inference.} At test time, to impute a missing view for sample $i$, we: (1) sample initial noise $\mathbf{z}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$; (2) construct conditioning $\mathbf{c}$ based on the scenario; (3) integrate the ODE $\frac{d\mathbf{z}_t}{dt} = v_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t, \mathbf{c})$ using Euler method with 10 steps. The resulting $\mathbf{z}_1$ serves as the imputed latent representation, enabling complete multi-view fusion and clustering.

\subsection{Soft Clustering}\label{clustering}

We employ soft cluster assignments based on cosine similarity between latent representations $\mathbf{z}_i$ and learnable centroids $\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\}_{k=1}^K$:
\[
q_{ik} = \frac{\exp(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_i^\top \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_k / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^{K} \exp(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_i^\top \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_j / \tau)}
\]
where $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_i$ and $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_k$ are $\ell_2$-normalized. Following the DEC paradigm \cite{xieUnsupervisedDeepEmbedding2016}, we define a sharpened target distribution $P$ and minimize $\mathcal{L}_{clu} = KL(P \| Q)$ to encourage high-confidence assignments.

\subsection{Overall Objective}\label{overall-objective}

The total objective integrates five loss terms:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{tot} = \mathcal{L}_{CFM} + \lambda_g \mathcal{L}_{GW} + \lambda_c \mathcal{L}_{clu} + \lambda_r \mathcal{L}_{rec} + \lambda_t \mathcal{L}_{con}
\]
where 
% $\mathcal{L}_{CFM}$ (flow matching), $\mathcal{L}_{GW}$ (structural alignment), and $\mathcal{L}_{clu}$ (soft clustering) are defined in previous sections; 
$\mathcal{L}_{rec} = \sum_v \|\mathbf{X}^{(v)} - g_{\psi_v}(\mathbf{z}^{(v)})\|^2$ preserves view-specific information; and $\mathcal{L}_{con}$ (InfoNCE) enhances cross-view consistency for \textbf{aligned data only} (set $\lambda_t=0$ for UMVC). Default hyperparameters: $\lambda_g=0.2$, $\lambda_c=1.0$, $\lambda_r=0.5$, $\lambda_t=0.3$ (aligned) or $0$ (unaligned). Note that all loss components except $\mathcal{L}_{con}$ are active in both aligned and unaligned scenarios; only the flow conditioning source differs (consensus representation for IMVC vs.\ cluster centroid for UMVC). See Table~\ref{tab:loss_config} in the appendix for details.

\subsection{Optimization}\label{optimization}

\textbf{Training Procedure.} We adopt a three-phase training strategy: (1) \textit{Warm-up I}: train encoder-decoder with $\mathcal{L}_{rec}$ to establish a stable latent manifold; (2) \textit{Warm-up II}: initialize centroids via K-Means and apply single-view DEC loss to develop cluster-separable representations; (3) \textit{Joint Training}: end-to-end optimization with $\mathcal{L}_{tot}$, periodically refining centroids via K-Means.

\textbf{Implementation.} Adam optimizer ($\text{lr}=10^{-3}$), gradient clipping (max norm 1.0), Euler ODE solver with 10 steps. The complete process is in Algorithm~\ref{alg:option}.

\begin{algorithm}[tb]
  \caption{End-to-End Training for OPTION}
  \label{alg:option}
  \begin{algorithmic}
    \STATE {\bfseries Input:} Multi-view data $\mathcal{X}$, Number of clusters $K$, Hyperparameters $\lambda_g, \lambda_c, \lambda_r, \lambda_t$, Alignment flag \texttt{is\_aligned}.
    \STATE {\bfseries Initialize:} Encoder-decoder parameters $\phi$, Vector field parameters $\theta$, Cluster centroids $\mathbf{M}$ via K-Means on initial embeddings.
    \FOR{epoch $= 1$ to $E$}
    \FOR{each mini-batch $\mathcal{B} \subset \mathcal{X}$}
        \STATE Encode views: $\mathbf{z}^{(v)} = f_{\phi_v}(\mathbf{x}^{(v)})$ for all views.
        \IF{\texttt{is\_aligned}}
            \STATE Compute consensus: $\mathbf{z}^{(c)} = \frac{1}{V}\sum_v \mathbf{z}^{(v)}$.
            \STATE Condition $\mathbf{c} \gets \mathbf{z}^{(c)}$.
        \ELSE
            \STATE \textit{// Unaligned: each view operates independently.}
            \STATE For each view $v$ and sample $i$:
            \STATE \quad $q_{ik}^{(v)} = \frac{\exp(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_i^{(v)\top} \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_k / \tau)}{\sum_j \exp(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_i^{(v)\top} \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_j / \tau)}$
            \STATE \quad $k^{*(v)}_i = \argmax_k q_{ik}^{(v)}$
            \STATE \quad Condition $\mathbf{c}_i^{(v)} \gets \boldsymbol{\mu}_{k^{*(v)}_i}$
            \STATE \textit{// No cross-view voting: index $i$ has no correspondence.}
        \ENDIF
        \STATE Sample noise $\mathbf{z}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and time $t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]$.
        \STATE Compute CFM Loss: $\mathcal{L}_{CFM} = \| v_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t, \mathbf{c}) - (\mathbf{z}_1 - (1-\sigma_{min})\mathbf{z}_0) \|^2$.
        \STATE Compute GW Alignment Loss $\mathcal{L}_{GW}$ on encoded latents.
        \STATE Compute Clustering Loss $\mathcal{L}_{clu} = KL(P_{\mathcal{B}} \| Q_{\mathcal{B}})$.
        \STATE Compute $\mathcal{L}_{rec}$; compute $\mathcal{L}_{con}$ only if \texttt{is\_aligned}.
        \STATE Update all parameters via Adam with gradient clipping.
    \ENDFOR
    \IF{epoch mod $T_{update} = 0$}
        \STATE Refine centroids $\mathbf{M}$ via K-Means on current embeddings.
    \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE {\bfseries Output:} Trained model, Cluster assignments $\argmax_k q_{ik}$.
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\textbf{Datasets.} We conduct experiments on five widely used multi-view benchmarks: Bdgp, Coil20, CUB, Handwritten and Nuswide.

\textbf{Baselines.} We compare OPTION with SOTA Incomplete/Unaligned MVC Incomplete/Unaligned MVC methods, including COMPLETER \cite{linCOMPLETERIncompleteMultiview2021}, SURE \cite{yangRobustMultiViewClustering2023}, DealMVC \cite{yangDealMVCDualContrastive2023}, the diffusion-based DCG \cite{zhangIncompleteMultiviewClustering2025a}, MRG-UMC \cite{xinMultilevelReliableGuidance2025}, CANDY \cite{guoRobustContrastiveMultiview2024}, MFLVC \cite{xuMultilevelFeatureLearning2022}, GCFAggMVC \cite{yanGCFAggGlobalCrossView2023}, FreeCSL \cite{daiImputationfreeAlignmentfreeIncomplete2025a}, ROLL \cite{sunROLLRobustNoisy2025} and PROTOCOL \cite{xuePROTOCOLPartialOptimal2025}.

\textbf{Implementation Details.} OPTION is implemented in PyTorch. We use an MLP-based encoder-decoder and a conditional flow matching network. The ODE solver uses the Euler method with $N=10$ steps. The hyperparameters $\lambda_{gw}$, $\lambda_{c}$, and $\lambda_{con}$ are tuned via grid search. All experiments are run on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. Evaluation metrics include Accuracy (ACC), Normalized Mutual Information (NMI), and Adjusted Rand Index (ARI).

\section{Preliminaries on Flow Matching}\label{app:flow_matching}

Flow Matching (FM) \cite{lipmanFlowMatchingGenerative2023a} provides a simulation-free framework for learning generative models by directly regressing a vector field that transports samples from a source distribution $p_0$ (typically Gaussian noise) to a target distribution $p_1$ (data). Unlike diffusion models that require iterative stochastic denoising, FM learns a deterministic continuous normalizing flow (CNF) via ordinary differential equations (ODEs).

\textbf{Continuous Normalizing Flow.} Given a time-dependent vector field $u_t: \mathbb{R}^d \times [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}^d$, the flow is defined by the ODE:
\[
\frac{d\mathbf{z}_t}{dt} = u_t(\mathbf{z}_t), \quad \mathbf{z}_0 \sim p_0, \quad t \in [0, 1]
\]
Solving this ODE forward from $t=0$ to $t=1$ generates samples: $\mathbf{z}_1 = \mathbf{z}_0 + \int_0^1 u_t(\mathbf{z}_t) dt$. The distribution evolves according to the continuity equation $\frac{\partial p_t}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (p_t u_t) = 0$, ensuring $p_0$ is transported to $p_1$.

\textbf{Conditional Flow Matching.} Directly learning $u_t$ requires access to $p_t$ at all intermediate times, which is intractable. The Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) objective \cite{lipmanFlowMatchingGenerative2023a} bypasses this by conditioning on data samples $\mathbf{x}_1 \sim p_1$. For each data point $\mathbf{x}_1$, we define a conditional probability path $p_t(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}_1)$ that interpolates between noise $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and $\mathbf{x}_1$. The conditional vector field $u_t(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}_1)$ generates straight trajectories:
\[
u_t(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}_1) = \frac{\mathbf{x}_1 - (1-\sigma_{min})\mathbf{x}_0}{1 - (1-\sigma_{min})t}, \quad \mathbf{z}_t = (1 - (1-\sigma_{min})t)\mathbf{x}_0 + t\mathbf{x}_1
\]
where $\sigma_{min} > 0$ is a small regularization constant. The neural network $v_\theta(\mathbf{z}, t)$ is trained via:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{CFM}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{z}_t} \| v_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t) - u_t(\mathbf{z}_t|\mathbf{x}_1) \|^2
\]
This enables efficient training without ODE simulation, and at inference, the learned vector field can be integrated using few-step ODE solvers (e.g., 10 Euler steps).
